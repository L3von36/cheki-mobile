Why Mahtem

Trust, but verify — instantly

Fake payment screenshots are a real problem in Ethiopian marketplaces. Mahtem closes that gap: it fetches the receipt straight from the bank's public endpoint on your device and compares what you were shown with what the bank actually recorded.

Scan it, paste it — or both at once

Point your camera at a receipt QR code, paste a reference number, or drop in a link you were sent over Telegram. Batch checking is built in, so a whole afternoon of market sales can be audited in one go — each receipt gets its own verdict.

Straight from the bank

No middleman re-typing your data. The app reads the official record on your device and tells you if the payment is genuine.

Private by design

Accounts and history stay on your phone, encrypted with keys sealed in the Android Keystore.

In your language

Fully localized in Amharic, Afaan Oromoo and Tigrinya, alongside English.

History & export

Every check is saved locally with its verdict — look back, batch-audit and share results.

Works where it matters

Small, fast and offline-graceful — built for real Ethiopian network conditions, not for a demo.

Zero-knowledge cloud, if you want it

The optional cloud backup stores only cryptographic digests and AES-256-GCM ciphertext — the server mathematically cannot read your identifiers, passwords or history. Your vault key is derived from your password and never leaves your phone.

How it works

Three steps to a stamped receipt

1

Scan

Open Mahtem and scan the QR code on the payment receipt — or paste the reference number or link you received.

2

Verify

Mahtem reads the official record from the bank and checks every detail: amount, parties, date and status.

3

Trust the stamp

Genuine payments get the ማህተም stamp with full receipt details. Anything odd gets a clear warning — before you hand over the goods.

Security

Your receipts belong to you

Verification happens on your device

Receipt checks go straight from your phone to the bank's official endpoint. Mahtem's servers are not in that path.

Passwords never leave the phone

Your password is stretched with PBKDF2 (120,000 iterations) on-device; only the derived key is ever sent, and it is useless as a password anywhere else.

Backups are ciphertext, nothing else

If you opt into cloud backup, the server stores AES-256-GCM ciphertext and SHA-256 digests — even a full server compromise leaks nothing readable.

Auditable, end to end

Both the app and the server are open source under the MIT license — anyone can verify exactly what leaves the phone.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Mahtem free?
Yes — receipt verification is free and needs no account. Mahtem Pro is an optional upgrade (300 ETB/month, paid by Telebirr) that adds encrypted cloud backup and sign-in across devices.
Does Mahtem store my receipts?
No. Your verification history lives only on your phone. If you opt into cloud backup, the server stores encrypted ciphertext it cannot decrypt — see the privacy policy for exactly what that means.
Do I need an account to verify receipts?
Never. Scanning and verifying work completely without an account. An account is only for the optional cloud backup.
Which banks and wallets are supported?
CBE, Telebirr, Bank of Abyssinia, M-Pesa, Dashen, Awash, Wegagen, Amhara Bank, Zemen Bank, CBE Birr, Siinqee Bank, Coopay and eBirr — with more on the way. Missing one? Open an issue.
Is Mahtem affiliated with the banks?
No. Mahtem is an independent, open-source tool. It simply reads the official public endpoints that the banks themselves publish for receipt verification — and shows you exactly what they recorded.
Where can I get the app?
Download the latest Android APK from GitHub Releases. The full source code is published under the MIT license.
